## The skills ladder

Every stage of the selection tests a skill that sits on top of the previous one. Picture five rungs:

1. **NOAI Prelim: recall.** Recognise a concept and answer in about half a minute, with no calculator.
2. **NOAI Final: implement.** Turn a known algorithm (K-means, gradient boosting, attention) into correct NumPy or PyTorch code inside a notebook with tests.
3. **NTU Selection Test: complete and improve.** Take a <Term def="A notebook from the organisers that already loads the data, trains a simple model and writes a valid submission. Your job is to fill its gaps and beat its score.">baseline notebook</Term> on a real dataset, fill in its missing lines, then improve its score, often with a pretrained model on a <Term def="Graphics processing unit: a chip that does the many small multiplications of deep learning in parallel, making training tens of times faster than on a CPU.">GPU</Term>.
4. **NTU training and camp: learn new model families.** CLIP, GANs, multilingual Transformers, speech models: new tools, learnt fast, on real hardware.
5. **IOAI: invent under constraints.** A story-style task you have never seen, a rule sheet that forbids the obvious approach, and a <Term def="A live ranking of submissions by their score on hidden test data.">leaderboard</Term>.

<SkillsLadder />

The rungs are not about difficulty alone. Look at the verbs: recall, implement, improve, learn, invent. A student who has only ever practised MCQs can reach rung 1 brilliantly and still freeze on rung 3, because nobody hands you the algorithm there; you have to decide what to try.

<KeyIdea>

Each rung needs the one below it: you cannot improve a training pipeline you could not write, or invent under constraints with tools you cannot use. Find the lowest rung where you still have gaps and practise **that** next.

</KeyIdea>

<Note title="Your NOAI tier is not the last word">

As you saw in [After NOAI](#/learn/road-to-ioai/after-noai), NTU re-ranks everyone it invites. Its stage tests rungs 3 to 5, which you can still build after the Final.

</Note>

## Where to practise

You will not find rung 3 to 5 practice in MCQ banks. These are the sources worth your time, roughly in the order to use them:

| Source | What it gives you | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NOAI past papers: the <a href={LINKS.final2026Repo}>2026 Final notebooks</a> and <a href={LINKS.noai2025Repo}>2025 notebooks</a> | Real Final tasks with solutions | Finishing rung 2 |
| Singapore's IOAI pre-selection and camp tasks (2024 and 2025), released as zips on AI Singapore's NOAI pages and listed on the [IOAI resources hub](https://ioai-official.org/resources/) | Baseline notebooks on CIFAR-10, Pascal VOC, survey data, tweets, GANs | Rung 3 and 4: the closest thing to the NTU tests |
| <a href={LINKS.ioaiGithub}>IOAI task repositories</a> (2024 to 2026) | Past IOAI tasks with their baseline notebooks | Rung 5, one task per week |
| <a href={LINKS.aicc}>AI Community Contest (AICC)</a> | Monthly IOAI-style contests run by IOAI alumni | Timed practice with a leaderboard |
| [Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions) Playground competitions | Small, friendly contests, mostly on tables of data | Tabular ML and leaderboard discipline |
| [Hugging Face Learn](https://huggingface.co/learn) | Free courses on Transformers, audio, computer vision and diffusion | Practice-only syllabus items |

You also need a GPU for rungs 3 to 5. Free cloud notebooks such as Google Colab and Kaggle Notebooks offer limited free GPU time, which is enough for small fine-tuning runs if you cache features and keep experiments short.

<ExamTip title="Practise in IOAI conditions">

IOAI 2026 ran in JupyterLab with one GPU, **no internet** apart from a whitelist of library documentation, PyTorch and scikit-learn but **no TensorFlow or Keras**, and an official in-platform assistant LLM with a short output limit. So practise in PyTorch, with the documentation open and AI autocomplete off, and time yourself.

</ExamTip>

**Project ideas** to fill gaps between contests (each uses free data):

- **Hawker-food photo classifier.** Fine-tune a pretrained ResNet with augmentation on a few hundred photos you take or find (vision, transfer learning).
- **Unlabelled-comment detector.** Fine-tune a small BERT on a labelled sample of comments, then add pseudo-labels from unlabelled ones (text, semi-supervised learning).
- **HDB resale prices.** Gradient boosting with group features on data.gov.sg's resale data, as in the 2025 camp's tabular task (tabular ML, feature engineering).
- **Spoken-digit or bird-call classifier.** Extract HuBERT or Whisper embeddings and train a small head (audio).
- **Photo-album search.** Embed your photos with CLIP and search them with sentences (multimodal, embeddings).

## A weekly plan to July

The dates are fixed, so plan backwards from them:

- **{RESULTS.date}:** NOAI results (award tiers only).
- **{SEL.date}:** NTU Selection Test. {SEL.detail}
- **{TRAIN.date}:** weekend training at NTU. {TRAIN.detail}
- **{CAMP.date}:** residential camp. {CAMP.detail} IOAI releases its at-home tasks in June, about a month before the contest; the team picked at camp works on them afterwards.
- **{IOAI.date}:** IOAI 2027 in Singapore, with a team of {FUNNEL[4].n} (see [After NOAI](#/learn/road-to-ioai/after-noai)).

<Analogy title="Training for a marathon">

Marathon plans are periodised: weeks of easy base mileage, then race-specific sessions at race pace, then a taper so you arrive fresh. Four months to IOAI split the same way.

</Analogy>

1. **Base (March).** Rest for a few days after the Final, then build rung 3 tools: a PyTorch training loop on a GPU, transfer learning, Hugging Face basics. One past Singapore selection task every week or two.
2. **Selection-specific (April to 8 May).** One past selection or camp task per week, rotating vision, text and tabular, plus a timed mini-contest every other week. Do not wait for the results in early April: four weeks is not enough to build GPU fluency from zero.
3. **Broaden (May and June).** If you are invited onwards, the NTU Saturdays and camp set the pace; add audio, multimodal and generative practice and one IOAI past task per week. Mark your school's mid-year exam weeks as rest.
4. **Home tasks and taper (late June).** The IOAI at-home tasks if you are on the team, a couple of light timed contests, and a rest-heavy final week.

<Mistake>

Practising only what you enjoy. A student who loves vision can do ten CV tasks and feel ready, then meet a Selection Test whose text task is worth as much. The plan builder's first check exists for this reason: each area at least twice before 8 May.

</Mistake>

## The coverage map: find and fill your gaps

The official study guidance for NOAI is to "review the IOAI syllabus". The latest published version (the IOAI 2026 syllabus; check the <a href={LINKS.ioai2027}>IOAI 2027 page</a> for a newer one) has four sections:

1. Foundational skills and classical machine learning,
2. Neural networks and deep learning,
3. Computer vision,
4. Natural language processing and audio.

Think of it as comparing your O-level syllabus checklist with what the A-level syllabus adds: you tick off what you have, and the gaps tell you what to study next. Each subtopic is labelled **Theory** (understand how and why it works), **Practice** (know when to use it and how to run it with a library) or **Both**. Two sentences in it matter for planning: topics not listed are excluded, and "breadth should be prioritized over depth".

<SyllabusCoverageGrid />

This course was built for NOAI, so it goes deepest on theory and from-scratch code. Filter the map to **Practice** and the dashed cells line up: GPU training, fine-tuning with PEFT, object detection, diffusion pipelines and the audio models. None is hard to understand once you have done this course; they simply need hours with the libraries (`transformers`, `peft`, `torchvision`, `diffusers`) that only practice gives.

The Prelim already asks about these practice-only topics, but only at the level of "what is it?". Two you must be able to define: **LoRA** freezes a weight matrix $W$ and trains only a small low-rank update $\Delta W = BA$, which makes fine-tuning cheap; **YOLO** is a single-stage, real-time object detector that predicts all boxes and classes in one forward pass.

At IOAI the same topics come back as "use it": fine-tune with LoRA under a GPU budget, or run a detector and parse its boxes. That is the gap the plan above is for.

<KeyIdea>

Breadth beats depth: a dashed cell in any of the four sections is a bigger risk than a missing detail in a section you already know. Use the coverage map to choose next month's practice tasks and reading.

</KeyIdea>

</ExamTip>
