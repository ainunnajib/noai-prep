The **National Olympiad in Artificial Intelligence (NOAI)** is Singapore's national AI competition for students. It is free, you need no AI experience to sit the first round, and the strongest students go on to represent Singapore at the **International Olympiad in AI**, which Singapore hosts in July 2027. This lesson is your map: the stages, the dates that matter, and how to get in.

## Who runs what

Four names come up again and again in this course:

- **NOAI** is the national competition. It has two rounds: a **Preliminary Round** (the "Prelim") that every eligible student can sit, and a **Final** for about the top 150.
- **<Term def="AI Singapore: the national AI programme, hosted by NUS. It organises both NOAI rounds.">AI Singapore (AISG)</Term>** organises both NOAI rounds.
- **<Term def="Nanyang Technological University. After NOAI, NTU runs the selection test and training that choose Singapore's IOAI team.">NTU</Term>** runs the selection test and training that turn NOAI finalists into a national team.
- **<Term def="International Olympiad in Artificial Intelligence: a yearly world contest in AI for school students, first held in 2024.">IOAI</Term>** is the international contest. It started in 2024 and moves to a new host country every year.

## The selection funnel

<Analogy title="Getting into the National Day Parade">

Thousands of people arrive at the stadium gates. A ticket check lets some through, a bag check comes next, and then you find your seat. There are fewer people at each checkpoint, and **each checkpoint checks something different**. NOAI works the same way.

</Analogy>

There are four checkpoints between you and the IOAI team:

1. **The Prelim** ({PRELIM.dateLabel}). Every eligible student whose school signs up: {PRELIM.questions} <Term def="Multiple-choice questions: each has four options and exactly one correct answer.">MCQs</Term> in {PRELIM.minutes} minutes.
2. **The Final** ({FINAL.dateLabel}). About the top 150 from the Prelim, ranked on total correct answers, with school seat quotas so that no single school takes too many seats (the quotas are not published): 20 MCQs plus 3 Python programming tasks. Ties for 150th place are resolved with predefined criteria, such as performance in particular domains and the time taken; where appropriate, all tied students may go through.
3. **NTU selection and training** (May to June 2027). About the top 60 finalists sit NTU's Selection Test; about 10 reach a week-long residential camp.
4. **The IOAI team** (4 to 10 July 2027, in Singapore). Between 4 and 8 Singapore Citizens and PRs.

<SelectionFunnel />

Look at the **Rewards** line on each band's card. **Each stage rewards a different skill**, so the way you prepare has to change as you go:

| Stage | What it rewards | How you train it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prelim | **Breadth and speed**: knowing a little about a lot, and recalling it in seconds | Short lessons, regular self-testing, timed MCQ sets |
| Final | **Implementation**: writing a classic algorithm from scratch in Python | Coding each algorithm yourself, without AI help |
| NTU and IOAI | **Modelling**: building, testing and improving a model on real data | Open-ended tasks and past IOAI problems |

<KeyIdea>

NOAI is a series of filters, and each one tests something new: breadth and speed (Prelim), then from-scratch coding (Final), then real-model building (NTU and IOAI). Train the skills in that order, but **start each one before you need it**.

</KeyIdea>

<Note title="Your award tier is not the last word">

NTU runs its own Selection Test and training and re-ranks everyone it invites. A student who won Bronze at NOAI 2025 went on to place 2nd in the world at IOAI 2025.

</Note>

<Mistake>

Treating the Prelim as the finish line. The Final rewards a different skill, and two weeks is not enough to learn it from scratch. Start the Final labs (from-scratch coding in NumPy and PyTorch, the two Python libraries the Final uses: NumPy for maths on arrays of numbers, PyTorch for neural networks) in January, while you are still revising for the Prelim.

</Mistake>

## Who can enter, and how to register

<Analogy title="Like an inter-school competition">

For an inter-school competition, your CCA teacher sends in the team list; you cannot just turn up on the day. NOAI registration also goes through your school.

</Analogy>

The rules for NOAI 2027:

- **MOE students.** Full-time students below age 20 in an MOE secondary school, IP school, JC, ITE or polytechnic can enter, **whatever their nationality**. You register through your school: each school appoints an NOAI teacher representative, and the school signs up with AI Singapore by **{SIGN_UP}**. Requests from individual MOE students are not accepted.
- **Everyone else who is eligible applies directly** by emailing **{CONTACT_EMAIL}** by **{WRITE_IN}**. That covers home-schooled Singapore Citizens and PRs; students between schools (for example, finished Sec 4 and waiting to start JC or poly); and private or international school students, who are considered case by case. Approved students sit the Prelim at the AI Singapore office at NUS and compete for the same 150 Final places.
- **It is free** for every student accepted.
- **Only Singapore Citizens and PRs** can be picked for the IOAI team.

## Where it leads: the IOAI syllabus

IOAI publishes a syllabus that every contest task must follow. The latest is the IOAI 2026 Syllabus, and AI Singapore's own advice is to "review the IOAI syllabus". It has four sections. Most of the words in the table will mean nothing to you yet, and that is expected: each one gets its own lesson later in the course.

| Section | Examples of what is in it |
| --- | --- |
| Foundational skills and classical ML | Python, NumPy, pandas, PyTorch; regression, decision trees, k-means, evaluation metrics |
| Neural networks and deep learning | Gradient descent, backpropagation, attention, Transformers, regularisation |
| Computer vision | Convolutions, segmentation with U-Net, GANs, CLIP, diffusion models |
| Language and audio | Text classification, BERT, language modelling, Whisper |

Each of its 60 topics is tagged **Theory** (understand how it works), **Practice** (know when to use it and how to call it in code) or **Both**. Two rules in it are worth remembering: "breadth should be prioritized over depth", and topics that are not listed are excluded. The NOAI Prelim adds three areas the IOAI syllabus does not list as sections of their own: **Mathematics**, **Generative AI** (which IOAI spreads across its vision and language sections) and **Problem solving** (debugging and system design). This course covers all of them; the last module, [Beyond NOAI: the road to IOAI 2027](#/learn/road-to-ioai/after-noai), goes deeper into the NTU and IOAI stages.
